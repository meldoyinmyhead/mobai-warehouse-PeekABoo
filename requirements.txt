SECTION 2: Warehouse Operations Flow
Objective: Build a mobile WMS (Warehouse Management System) managing end-to-end flow.

ROLES:

ADMIN: Can override AI decisions.

SUPERVISOR: Can override AI decisions.

EMPLOYEE: Abstraction layer; sees only final validated orders.

ORDER TYPES:

Command Order: Initiates the receipt process.

Preparation Order: Specifies products/quantities for delivery preparation.

Picking Order: Guides picking into racks.

Delivery Validation/Failure: Records outcome.

CORE OPERATIONS:

Receipt: Receiving merchandise.

Transfer: Moving products to storage.

Picking: Selecting and placing products in picking racks.

Delivery: Final expedition of products.

OPERATIONAL STEP-BY-STEP (PAGE 3):

Receipt: Triggered by 'Command Order' from office. Products logged and validated.

Transfer: Move products to storage floors (long-term holding).

Preparation: Triggered by 'Preparation Order'. Generated by AI forecasting (1 day in advance).

Picking: Triggered by 'Picking Order'. Generated by AI optimization (shortest distance logic).

Delivery: Execution followed by Delivery Validation/Failure.

PART 5: OPTIMIZATION CONCEPT (PAGES 5-6)

GOAL: Minimize operational effort, maximize efficiency.

5.1 DISTANCE OPTIMIZATION
Must compute minimum travel distance for:

From Receipt Zone -> Storage Location.

From Storage Location -> Expedition Track.

Objectives:

Reduce Employee/Chariot walking distance.

Reduce Product handling time.

Reduce Operational congestion (Chariot paths should not interfere).

5.2 OPTIMIZATION FACTORS
Variables the AI must consider:

Product Weight: Heavy products closer to ground/expedition.

Product Demand Frequency: Frequent picks nearer to picking racks.

Delivery Frequency: High-turnover SKUs minimize storage-to-delivery distance.

Reception Frequency: Frequent receipts -> accessible storage slots.

Slot Availability: Dynamic assignment based on free space.

Allowed Algorithms: Shortest path, clustering, heuristic placement, or AI-driven learning models.

PART 6: WAREHOUSE STRUCTURE & DOMAIN (PAGES 6-7)

6.1 DOMAIN DEFINITIONS

SKU: Unique product identifier.

Stock Ledger: Record of movements (receipts, transfers, picks, deliveries).

Chariot: Carts used for moving products (Capacity is IGNORED).

6.2 WAREHOUSE STRUCTURE

Target: Depot B7.

Floors: Ground (0A) + Storage Levels (1 to 4).

Addressing Format:

Picking Locations (Ground): B7-0A-XX-YY (0A=Ground, XX=Row, YY=Column).

Storage Locations (Upper): B7-NZ-CW (NZ=Level e.g., N1, CW=Slot Code e.g., C7).

Storage Physics:

No racks in upper storage.

Products stored on palettes (1 square meter each).

Volume is NOT a metric; focus on AREA and Slot Availability.

PART 9: AI INTEGRATION TASKS (PAGE 13)

Requirement: Design a multi-service agent.

8.1 FORECASTING SERVICE

Analyze historical stock/delivery data.

Predict required products/quantities.

Trigger preparation 1 day in advance.

8.2 STORAGE OPTIMIZATION SERVICE

Determine optimal storage location after receipt.

Input: Weight, demand freq, slot availability, distance.

Output: Exact floor and slot assignment.

8.3 PICKING OPTIMIZATION SERVICE

Generate optimized Picking Orders.

Compute shortest routes.

Prioritize high-freq SKUs near expedition.

8.4 OVERRIDE HANDLING

Log all overrides by Supervisor/Admin.

Employees only execute the final decision (AI or Overridden).
